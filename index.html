Activity 4: Complete Guide & 15 Regret Transformations

Grammar Reference: How to Use Wish and If Only

Both wish and if only express regrets or desires for reality to be different. If only carries stronger emotion and emphasis than I wish. Notice how the verb shifts one tense back into the past:

1. Present Situation We Want Different
Structure: wish / if only + Past Simple (or were)

• Reality: I live far from school.
• Wish: "I wish I didn't live so far from school."
• Wish: "If only I were better at public speaking."

2. Regret About a Past Event
Structure: wish / if only + Past Perfect (had + V3)

• Reality: I forgot to set my alarm, so I missed the bus.
• Wish: "I wish I had set my alarm."
• Wish: "If only I hadn't forgotten my alarm!"

3. Present Ability or Possibility
Structure: wish / if only + could + base verb

• Reality: I can't play the piano.
• Wish: "I wish I could play the piano."
• Wish: "If only we could travel this summer!"

4. Annoying Habits / Desired External Change
Structure: wish / if only + someone/something + would + base verb

• Reality: My brother always interrupts me.
• Wish: "I wish my brother wouldn't interrupt me."
• Note: Do not use I wish I would... for your own actions.

Reading Passage: The Bridge

The bridge was a thin gray line above the gorge, and Robert lay behind a pine tree and watched it. The morning was cold. Below him the river made a hard, steady noise over the rocks. He did not like the noise. It covered the sounds he needed to hear.

Pilar sat beside him and cleaned her knife on her sleeve. She said nothing for a long time. Then she said, “The old man will come at dawn with the horses.”

“If he comes,” Robert said.

“He will come. He is stubborn, and that is why he is still alive.”

Robert looked at the guard at the end of the bridge. The guard was young and kept stamping his feet to stay warm. He was only a boy, and Robert wished he were not there. But wishing was a thing for other men in other places.

“If only we had more dynamite,” he said. “If only the others had not left in the night.”

“They were afraid,” Pilar said. “Fear is not a crime. It is only a weight.”

“I know.” He touched the small wire in his pocket. “I wish I could say I was not afraid too.”

She laughed softly, without any joy. “Then you are an honest man. That is rare.”

The wind turned and brought the smell of smoke from the village. Somewhere a dog barked twice and stopped. Robert counted the minutes on his watch. Four hours remained. He thought of the girl in the cave, and of how little time two people can steal from a war.

He took a breath and checked the wire again. The work had to be done well, because there would be no second chance. In the pines behind him a bird began to sing, as if nothing in the world had changed.
